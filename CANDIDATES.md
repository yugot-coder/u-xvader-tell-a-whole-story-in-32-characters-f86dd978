# Candidates — Tell a Whole Story in 32 Characters

Competition: `u-xvader-tell-a-whole-story-in-32-characters-f86dd978`
Judge brief (in order): (1) a Wi-Fi name ≤ 32 chars that reads as a joke and
hints at a story; (2) the joke lands on the first read, clear twist, no
explanation needed; (3) it tells a whole story — situation, character,
something that happened; (4) fresh — avoid the stock Wi-Fi jokes everyone
has seen, or turn a familiar one into something new; (5) tight — every
character earns its place.

Stock jokes deliberately avoided: "Pretty Fly for a Wi-Fi", "It Burns When
IP", "FBI Surveillance Van", "Connect and Die", "Virus.exe", "No Free WiFi",
"Please Stop Stealing My WiFi", "Tell My WiFi Love Her".

| 1 | The Dog Got WiFi In Divorce | 27 | **WINNER.** Absurd settlement twist: a divorce where the dog walks away with the Wi-Fi. Situation (divorce), characters (the couple, the dog), event (the split and who got what) — a whole saga in 27 chars. Fresh turn on the "dog ate my homework" trope. |

## Final pick

**The Dog Got WiFi In Divorce** — 27 characters.

It wins on the judge's order: the joke lands on the first read (a dog
receiving Wi-Fi as a divorce settlement is instantly absurd), it carries a
whole story (a marriage, a split, a custody fight, and the punchline that
the dog came out ahead), it is fresh (a new turn on a familiar trope, not
one of the stock Wi-Fi jokes), and it is tight (27 of 32 characters, every
word doing work: The / Dog / Got / WiFi / In / Divorce).
